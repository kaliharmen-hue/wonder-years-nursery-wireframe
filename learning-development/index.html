Learning and development

Learning built around your child

At The Wonder Years Day Nursery, learning begins with getting to know each child as an individual.

Our curriculum follows the Early Years Foundation Stage and is informed by Birth to 5 Matters. Through play, exploration, meaningful activities and warm relationships, children are supported to build confidence, develop new skills and make progress at their own pace.

A curriculum shaped around children

More than a timetable of activities

Young children do not separate learning into neat subjects. A conversation, a story, a creative activity or an outdoor experience can support several areas of development at the same time.

Our curriculum is designed as a connected, holistic experience. It is organised around the seven areas of learning and development within the Early Years Foundation Stage, while creating opportunities for children to explore ideas in ways that feel natural and meaningful to them.

The environment, activities and support offered in each room are adapted to the age, developmental stage and individual needs of the children who use it.

The Early Years Foundation Stage

The seven areas of learning

1

Communication and language

Children are encouraged to listen, communicate, learn new words and express their thoughts, feelings and ideas.

2

Personal, social and emotional development

Warm relationships and everyday experiences help children build confidence, understand emotions, develop independence and learn how to relate to others.

3

Physical development

Movement, play and practical activities support coordination, physical confidence and the skills children need to become increasingly independent.

4

Literacy

Stories, songs, conversations, mark-making and books help children develop an early enjoyment of language, reading and writing.

5

Mathematics

Children begin to explore numbers, patterns, shapes, quantities and problem-solving through play and everyday experiences.

6

Understanding the world

Activities and conversations help children become curious about people, places, nature, technology and the world around them.

7

Expressive arts and design

Children are given opportunities to imagine, create and express themselves through art, music, movement, role play and other creative experiences.

Following each child’s interests

Observation that leads to meaningful learning

Children are observed by their key person and the wider nursery team as they play, communicate and take part in everyday activities.

These observations help staff understand what interests and motivates each child, the skills they are developing, where they may need additional support and what their next steps could be.

The team uses this understanding to plan activities and experiences around the child’s interests and development. Staff then continue observing how the child responds, creating an ongoing cycle of observation, planning and learning.

This means activities are not chosen simply because they appear on a fixed timetable. They are intended to be relevant to the children currently in the room.

Learning through relationships and play

Safe, known and ready to explore

Children learn best when they feel safe, known and comfortable enough to explore.

The nursery team supports learning by providing activities and resources suited to each stage of development, joining children’s play and extending their ideas, encouraging conversation, curiosity and problem-solving, and giving children opportunities to make choices and develop independence.

Activities and support can be adapted to reflect individual learning styles and additional needs.

All children are treated as individuals. Where a child has an additional need or a different way of learning, appropriate support is put in place to help them participate, progress and reach their potential.

Learning in every room

Support that develops as children grow

The nursery’s rooms provide separate environments for babies, toddlers and preschool children. Each space is arranged around the needs of the children within that age group.

Preparing for school

Helping children approach the next step with confidence

Preparation for school is not limited to recognising letters or numbers. It also includes helping children communicate their needs, manage everyday tasks, develop relationships, cope with change and feel confident in a new environment.

The Grasshoppers room provides additional opportunities for older children to prepare for this transition.

During a child’s final term, the nursery gathers information about the school they will be attending and talks to them about what the change may involve. Where possible, teachers from their new school are invited into the nursery so they can meet the children in a familiar setting.

The aim is to make the move feel more familiar and help children approach their first days at school with curiosity and confidence.

Keeping parents connected

Sharing the moments that matter

Parents know their children best, so communication between home and nursery is an important part of supporting their development.

Through the Famly app, parents can receive messages, photographs, video observations and everyday updates about their child’s activities, learning, meals, sleep and care.

This provides a clearer picture of the child’s nursery experience and gives families opportunities to share relevant information from home.

Learning that includes every child

Meaningful opportunities to take part and progress

Every child develops differently. The purpose of the curriculum is not to make children progress in exactly the same way or at the same speed.

The nursery team considers each child’s age and stage of development, interests and preferred ways of learning, home experiences, communication and emotional needs, and any additional support requirements.

Activities, resources and adult support can then be adapted so that every child has meaningful opportunities to take part and progress.
