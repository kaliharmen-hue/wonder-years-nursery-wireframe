Complaints Policy and Procedure

Last reviewed: August 2026

At The Wonder Years Day Nursery, we aim to give every child the best possible start and to provide a high standard of care and education.

We recognise that parents and carers may sometimes have a concern or feel dissatisfied with something that has happened. We want families to feel able to raise concerns openly, and we will work with them to resolve matters as fairly and promptly as possible.

This policy explains how concerns and complaints are handled.

1. Concerns and complaints

For the purposes of this policy, a concern is a worry or doubt about an issue where reassurance, clarification or action is being sought. For example, a parent may be worried about their child’s emotional wellbeing at nursery.

A complaint is an expression of dissatisfaction about something the nursery has done, or has failed to do. For example, a parent may be dissatisfied with the circumstances surrounding an injury their child received.

We believe it is in everyone’s interests to resolve concerns and complaints at the earliest appropriate stage.

2. Stage 1: Raise the concern with your child’s key person

If you have a concern about your child’s care or education, please raise it with your child’s key person as soon as possible.

The concern should be discussed openly and honestly. Where possible, the key person and parent or carer will agree what can be done to resolve the issue.

If the key person cannot resolve the concern, it will be referred to the Nursery Manager or Deputy Manager.

If your concern relates to your child’s key person and you do not feel comfortable raising it with them directly, you can speak to the Nursery Manager instead.

3. Stage 2: Speak to the Nursery Manager

If the concern has not been resolved at Stage 1, it should be referred to the Nursery Manager.

The Manager will arrange to speak with you in person or by telephone and will listen to and discuss the concern with you.

A record will be made of:

  • the concern raised;
  • relevant information discussed;
  • any agreed action; and
  • the outcome.

If the matter cannot be resolved at this stage, you will be invited to make a formal complaint in writing.

4. Stage 3: Formal written complaint

Formal complaints should be addressed to the Nursery Manager in writing. Complaints can be sent by email or letter.

The nursery will acknowledge receipt of a formal complaint within 48 hours.

The complaint will then be investigated. Depending on the circumstances, this may include discussions or meetings with the parent or carer and relevant staff, students or volunteers.

Throughout the process, the nursery will seek to deal with the complaint fairly and to rebuild a positive working relationship wherever possible.

The nursery will notify the complainant of the outcome of its investigation, and any action taken or planned, within 28 days of receiving the complaint.

The outcome will be provided in writing for formal written complaints.

5. Stage 4: Review by the nursery owner

If you are not satisfied with the outcome of the formal complaint, you should tell the Nursery Manager that you wish the decision to be reviewed.

The Nursery Manager will notify the nursery owner.

The owner will review the complaint and the investigation already carried out and may contact you to ensure they fully understand the issues you have raised.

The nursery will then explain the outcome of the review and any further action to be taken.

6. Complaints involving safeguarding or a person in a position of trust

Safeguarding concerns take priority over the normal complaints process.

If a complaint or concern relates to possible harm to a child, or to the conduct of a person in a position of trust within the nursery, it will be referred to the Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) within one working day and the nursery’s Safeguarding Policy will be followed.

Where appropriate, the nursery may seek advice from or make a referral to the relevant safeguarding authorities.

If you believe a child is in immediate danger, call 999.

If you are worried that a child may be at risk but the situation is not an immediate emergency, you can contact the Sandwell safeguarding team on 0121 569 3100 or West Midlands Police on 101.

7. Contacting Ofsted

Parents and carers should normally raise concerns with the nursery first and allow the nursery an opportunity to complete its complaints procedure.

However, you can contact Ofsted if:

  • you remain dissatisfied after the nursery has considered your complaint;
  • you believe the nursery is not meeting its statutory or registration requirements; or
  • your concern is of a nature that you do not feel able to raise directly with the nursery.

Ofsted regulates and inspects registered childcare providers. It cannot resolve individual disputes between a parent and a childcare provider, but it can consider concerns about whether a provider is meeting its regulatory requirements.

8. New concerns raised during an investigation

If a parent or carer raises a new and separate concern while another complaint is already being investigated, the new matter will normally be treated as a separate concern or complaint.

The relevant stages and timescales in this policy will apply to the new matter. Its outcome may be communicated separately from the original complaint.

9. Complaint records

The nursery keeps a written record of complaints and their outcomes.

The record may include:

  • the name of the person raising the concern or complaint;
  • the nature of the concern or complaint;
  • the investigation undertaken;
  • the outcome;
  • action taken or planned; and
  • relevant correspondence or meeting notes.

The nursery’s current retention policy is to keep complaint records for six years from the date they are recorded.

Records are stored securely and are handled in accordance with the nursery’s Privacy Notice and data-protection obligations. Ofsted may ask to see complaint records as part of its regulatory functions.

10. Outside advice and support

Where further advice or action is needed, the nursery may contact an appropriate external organisation. Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

  • Sandwell early years services;
  • Sandwell children’s safeguarding services;
  • Ofsted;
  • the Local Authority Designated Officer (LADO);
  • the police or other safeguarding partners;
  • the nursery’s insurer or professional advisers; or
  • legal advisers.

The organisation contacted will depend on the nature of the concern.

11. Our commitment

The nursery will:

  • take concerns and complaints seriously;
  • listen to parents and carers;
  • deal with complaints fairly and respectfully;
  • investigate formal complaints appropriately;
  • keep suitable records;
  • protect confidentiality as far as possible;
  • prioritise children’s safety and welfare; and
  • use concerns and complaints as an opportunity to review and improve practice.
